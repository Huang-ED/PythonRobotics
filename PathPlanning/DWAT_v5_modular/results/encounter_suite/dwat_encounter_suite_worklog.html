Phase 1 · before building

Investigation

Every design decision downstream came out of one of these measurements, which is why they came first.

  1. Read the v5_modular pipeline, the dwa_average_weighted_side cost function, map_manager's motion models, and the imported-scene format spec.
  2. Extracted the reference ego trajectory with timestamps from the v20.7.8-2 video3_lrshift log.
  3. Computed the Theta* global path: (70,90)→(48,55)→(44,46)→(47,39)→(66,30)→(69,27)→(60,10).
  4. Ray-cast port and starboard water clearance along the whole route — surfaced the 45-vs-8 unit asymmetry that forced the open-water control map.
  5. Re-ran video3 with dynamic_obstacles: [] and found the shipped obstacles are inert — 0.01 units of difference across 206 s.
  6. Measured the channel pinch at t≈167 s: 1.21 units of clearance against a ~1.215 threshold.
  7. Verified waypoint-mode obstacles hold exact constant velocity — the assumption the whole construction rests on.
  8. Read the cost function and established it has no port/starboard asymmetry, later confirmed by measurement.
Phase 2 · tooling

Three new tools

adapters/encounter_suite.py
Generator. Collision-course construction; course and bearing parameterization with a quadratic solver that refuses impossible geometries; CPA offsetting for the null case; COLREGs sector classification; navigability check against real coastlines; target-vs-target collision check; meeting-spacing warnings; --combine mode; manifest output.
adapters/encounter_report.py
Scoring. Surface-to-surface clearance, time of CPA, pass side and relative bearing, deviation from the obstacle-free baseline, delay to goal, minimum speed within the encounter window.
adapters/encounter_plot.py
Contact sheets, rendered from run logs rather than saved frames — so figures rebuild without re-simulating.

Targets sailing over islands

My first video3 pass produced two targets whose tracks crossed land. They would still have run and produced numbers.

Navigability check, then a sweep

Added the check, then swept every (course, speed) pair against the real coastline instead of guessing replacements.

Caught by the check I'd just written

Phase 6 · results

What the suite surfaced

Finding 01

No port/starboard rule

Mirrored pairs identical to two decimals: 0.57/0.58, 0.76/0.77, 0.94/0.94. Found by reading the cost function, then measured in the symmetric basin.

These 9 numbers are the COLREGs "before".
Finding 02

No safety domain

Clearances 0.35–1.22 units, traceable to max_side_weight_dist: 2.0 against 8.0 for static obstacles — a moving vessel gets a quarter the influence radius of a rock.

2.0 → 4 / 6 / 8 is the next sweep.
Finding 03

A long horizon can make a case unsolvable

The overtaken cases abort on iteration 0. Mechanism confirmed by falsification: predT30 fails, predT10 reaches goal, predT5 fails differently.

Not about overtaking — about the horizon.
Finding 04

Margin degrades as traffic stacks

The three-vessel simultaneous scene reached 0.35 units — tighter than any single encounter. The manoeuvre that clears the first two leaves less room for the third.

Consistent with Finding 02: no reserve.
Finding 05

Some encounters cannot occur on a real route

video3's Slot B admits no starboard crossing at all — the traffic would have to sail off the shore. Which encounters a route can produce is itself a property of the route.

Now checked, not assumed.
Noted in passing

Two repo hazards

algorithm.py:96 is an uncapped while True, so a non-converging run spins until the shell timeout. And .gitignore's bare figs* rule silently ignores every run log under Logs/.

Neither changed without your say-so.
