Where it started

The existing obstacles were doing nothing at all

Before designing anything I re-ran video3_lrshift.json with dynamic_obstacles: [] and compared it against the shipped run. The two trajectories are identical to within 0.01 units across the full 206 s, step for step.

The three circling obstacles never influence the ego. Whatever that scenario was testing, it was not avoidance. That single measurement reframed the task: the problem is not tuning obstacle placement, it is that hand-placement gives you no way to know whether an encounter happened. Everything downstream follows from deciding to place targets by construction instead.

The construction

Making a collision course, not a near miss

Take an obstacle-free reference run. At time t_meet the ego is at M with heading h and speed ve. Give the target a course χ and speed vt, and spawn it so that it arrives at M at the same instant:

P₀ = M − vt · χ̂ · t_meet

then, with both holding constant velocity:
target(t) − ego(t) = (t_meet − t) · ( ve·ĥ − vt·χ̂ )

That difference has a constant direction and a shrinking magnitude — the textbook definition of a constant-bearing, decreasing-range collision course. The encounter is guaranteed, not hoped for. Verified numerically: every collision case measures CPA 0.000 at exactly t_meet.

The same algebra gives the no-action control for free. Offsetting the spawn perpendicular to that fixed direction sets the closest approach to exactly that offset, because the along-track component is the part that collapses to zero. The null case measures 6.000 against a planned 6.0.

One consequence worth stating plainly: the target's relative bearing is an output, not an input. It falls out of χ and vt. That matters because bearing is what COLREGs is actually written in terms of, so every case is emitted with its bearing and rule sector attached, and the geometry can be checked rather than assumed.

Decision log

What the evidence forced

Each of these was a change of direction caused by a measurement, not a preference. Left: what turned up. Right: what it made me do.

Measured

DWA re-linearises every step

The planner extrapolates each target forward at constant velocity over the prediction horizon. A circling target's prediction is wrong within about one sample interval.

So

Straight-line targets only

Targets are emitted as waypoint motion with a single long leg. Verified to hold exact constant velocity and never double back. Circles would measure prediction error, not avoidance.

Measured

video3's water is 4:1 asymmetric

Ray-casting the route: on the long approach, starboard has 45 units of water and port has 8–16. Later the ratio inverts.

So

Built an open-water control basin

A turn to starboard on video3 may be the land's doing, not the planner's. Mirrored pairs only mean something in symmetric water — so the core suite runs in a featureless basin, and video3 became a separate confinement tier.

Measured

The route clears a pinch by 1.21 units

With no dynamic obstacles at all, the ego passes (68.0, 27.7) at t≈167 s with 1.21 units to spare against a ~1.22 collision threshold.

So

Excluded t = 160–175 entirely

Anything placed there tests the pinch, not the encounter. It also ruled out a third slot I had planned. "Pinch" is my own shorthand, not a term from the codebase: the stretch where land squeezes the navigable channel down to almost nothing.

This zero margin is also what makes plain video3.json fail on v20.7.8-2 — established by isolating the variable, not inferred. See below.

Caught

My first video3 targets sailed over islands

Two of five had tracks crossing land — physically impossible traffic that would still have run and produced numbers.

So

Added a navigability check, then swept

Rather than guess replacements, I swept every (course, speed) pair against the real coastline and kept only what floats. Slot A admits no port crossing at speed; Slot B admits no starboard crossing at all — starboard there is the shore.

Ambiguity

"90° crossing" has two readings

A 90° course crossing at target speed 0.4 puts the target at a bearing of 38.7°. Generators usually mean the first; COLREGs means the second.

So

Implemented both, defaulted to course

Bearing-mode solves a quadratic for the course. It also exposes a real constraint: holding a bearing abeam needs vt ≥ ve, and anything abaft the beam needs strictly faster — a slower vessel behind you can never close. Impossible geometries are refused, not fudged.

Measured

Detection range is ~25 units on a 103-unit route

predict_time_obstacle 30 s × ~0.9 closing speed. Targets sit inside the cost horizon for ~50 s before CPA.

So

One encounter per scenario file

Slots need ≥60 s separation, capping this route at two or three. Packing several into one run saves runs and confounds every result, so each case is isolated and shares an identical ego state.

Findings

Three things the suite surfaced

Finding 01 · confirmed twice

No port/starboard rule exists

The dynamic-obstacle cost in dwa_average_weighted_side is a spatio-temporal clearance run through a symmetric barrier. It is mirror-symmetric by construction. I found this by reading the cost function; the control basin then measured it.

COLREGs compliance is currently 50% by accident — the stand-on cases look correct only because doing nothing happens to be right there.

These 9 numbers are your "before".
Finding 02 · actionable

No safety domain, and the knob is named

Clearances cluster at 0.43–1.22 units — a third to one ship-length. That is max_side_weight_dist: 2.0: the side cost is zero beyond 2 units of the target's track, so the planner has no reason to want more room.

Static obstacles get max_obstacle_cost_dist: 8.0 — four times the influence radius of a moving vessel. The ego avoids hull contact, not close quarters.

2.0 → 4 / 6 / 8 is the next sweep.
Finding 03 · hard failure

A long horizon can make a case unsolvable

Both overtaken cases abort on iteration 0. At t=0 the ego is at rest, so the dynamic window admits only v ∈ [0, 0.02] — one acceleration step — yet each candidate is scored holding that velocity across the full 30 s horizon.

The planner predicts itself crawling 0.6 units while the target advances 18 and passes through it. Every candidate scores as colliding; nothing is left to choose.

predT30 fails · predT10 reaches goal · predT5 fails differently

Why plain video3 fails: isolating the variable

The claim that the pinch causes it started as an inference from three facts that merely fit together — the failure lands inside the pinch, the obstacle-free run survives it by ~0.005 units, and video3_lrshift differs only in obstacle 3's centre. That is circumstantial: obstacle 3 was never tested on its own.

So it was tested. Two controlled variants, everything else held constant:

Obstacle 3 removed (1 and 2 kept) → goal reached, 206.0 s. Obstacle 3 alone (1 and 2 removed) → fails at t=169.6 s at (67.32, 28.20) — the identical time and position as plain video3.json.

Necessary and sufficient. At the moment of failure that obstacle sat 1.99 units of surface clearance from the ego, against a max_side_weight_dist of exactly 2.0: it had just entered the cost's influence radius, in the one place on the route where the ego has no lateral room to answer.

Why finding 03 is not about overtaking

I had a mechanism hypothesis and wanted it falsifiable rather than asserted, so I re-ran the identical scenario at shorter horizons. At predT10 it reaches the goal — clearance 0.91, deviation 2.94, speed held at 0.50. At predT5 it fails with a different error entirely (No admissible (v, ω) pairs found in dynamic window).

So the horizon, not the encounter, is what makes it unsolvable. This will bite any case where a target is inbound on the ego's present position while the ego is slow — overtaking is merely the geometry that guarantees it. Given your existing predT5–30 sweep, the point is that horizon length is not only a cost/accuracy tradeoff: it can render situations infeasible that a shorter horizon handles.

What shipped

Three tools, two maps, twenty-one scenarios

adapters/encounter_suite.py
Generator. Course or bearing parameterization, collision-course construction, CPA offsetting, COLREGs sector classification, navigability check against the real coastline, manifest output.
adapters/encounter_report.py
Scoring. Surface-to-surface clearance, time of CPA, pass side and relative bearing, deviation from the obstacle-free baseline, delay to goal, minimum speed within the encounter window.
adapters/encounter_plot.py
Contact sheet. One panel per case: ego track against baseline, target track, closest-approach marker.
configs/scenarios/openwater_baseline.json
EnvData/openwater/openwater_100.npy
The symmetric control basin — featureless 100×100, blocked outer ring, straight north–south route. The ego starts at y=85 rather than y=92 specifically so the overtaken case has room astern.
configs/scenarios/owenc_*.json
configs/scenarios/v3enc_*.json
11 open-water cases and 10 video3 cases, plus two manifests recording each case's intended geometry, bearing, sector and spawn.
README.md
Design rationale, the two maps and why both exist, the course-vs-bearing distinction, and the predT finding written up as a known result.

Everything is uncommitted on master. The one change to existing behaviour is none — no planner or simulation code was modified.

One caveat to keep standing

The side at CPA column is not yet evidence of anything. With deviations of 1.7–3.0 units, which side the target ends up on falls out of the encounter geometry rather than any decision the planner made. It is recorded because it is the baseline you will diff against once a side rule exists — not because it is a compliance result today.
